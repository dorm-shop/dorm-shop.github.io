From seeds and microbes to insects and mammals, organisms use dormancy to survive periods when conditions are unfavorable for growth or reproduction. By slowing or pausing life processes, dormancy protects organisms and allows them to resume activity when conditions improve. Dormant individuals and their traits can persist for extended periods, forming a reservoir of biological information. This stored diversity provides a form of memory that helps living systems remain resilient in fluctuating environments.

"A unifying framework for dormancy across scales" will convene researchers to develop shared language, connect models, and identify research priorities across domains and scales. Sponsored by the [National Science Foundation (NSF)](https://www.nsf.gov/) and organized in partnership with [ASM](https://asm.org/), this in-person workshop will explore how the principles of dormancy can guide technological innovation and strengthen the bioeconomy, from storing living materials for advanced manufacturing to making biological supply chains more resilient. Ultimately, a deeper understanding of dormancy could allow us to control when organisms enter and leave dormant states, preserve biological materials, and support the resilience of entire ecosystems.
